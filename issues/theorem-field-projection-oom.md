Found in the datarepr v1 round (2026-08-15, docs/datarepr-design.md §1.4 as-landed note 7): projecting a theorem-valued field off an assembled record VALUE (e.g. `ListTok().Cons_abs`) exhausts memory and dies on the ulimit — a user-reachable SIGABRT, not a slow check. The datarepr elaboration works around it by reaching through the generated namespace (`Repr_N::Cons_abs()`), which is why nothing in the landed tests hits it. Repro shape: any record with a proof-valued field, assemble a value, project the proof field. Likely a conversion/unfolding blowup where the projection forces the whole record value; worth a minimized repro before any fix. Related: [[datarepr-separation]].

## Log

It is also now a PREREQUISITE for something wanted: repr-access-spellings' dot-shaped reference form, and any future reading of 'BinC.Succ_inv' as an expression rather than as a slot key, are blocked until this is fixed.

- 2026-08-15: The obligation-records round shipped without designing around this (2026-08-15). docs/obligation-records-design.md §1.3 was rewritten per the methodology verdict: the namespace rule now stands on its own three merits — the statement is source text before it is a term (which is what lets a '?' and the listing print something a human can read), laws must be reachable before the record value they are fields of exists, and one name per obligation is what the provenance/listing machinery keys on — and this bug is named as a bug to be fixed, with the note that the rule would be the rule anyway once it is. So nothing landed this round depends on the bug staying unfixed, and nothing landed this round is a workaround for it. Still unfixed, still user-reachable, still blocking repr-access-spellings' dot-shaped reference form.
- 2026-08-15: FIXED (2026-08-15), diagnosis first. The abort was **annotation duplication in `project_field`'s Σ-spine walk**, not conversion and not the record's size. `π_j` is `fst(sndʲ v)`, and each `Fst`/`Snd` node carries that level's Σ components (`fst_ty`, `snd_ty`) as annotations; the walk substitutes `π_j` into the tail to get level `j+1`, so every occurrence of field `j` in a later field's type inlines a node carrying the whole remaining record type — and *those* annotations are the payload one level down. Size is a product of the per-level occurrence counts: exponential in the field index.

Measured before touching anything. Instrumenting the walk (RSS and elapsed per step) put every microsecond in one line — `snd.apply_local(id.extended(proj))`; the `whnf` above it never exceeded 6 µs. RSS per step on a synthetic chain: 9.9 MB → 58 MB → 303 MB → dead, i.e. ~5-6× per entry, and on the reported record the walk died at entry 8 of 11. A scaling probe (a record whose `f_i` mentions `f_{i-1}` twice) checks in 11 ms at one field, 0.9 s at two, and aborts at three. Kernel-side counters ruled out the obvious suspects: the substitution visited 1.5 M nodes while the payload was inserted **four times**, so the cost was inside the copy, not in the number of occurrences — `subst.rs` shares unchanged subtrees and skips locally-closed ones, but a payload inserted under `k > 0` binders is weakened at insertion (`SubstLocal::local_var`), and a payload carrying a substitution tail — every global reference has one — is never skippable (`bounds::LocalUse::has_tail`), so each insertion deep-copies the payload including its annotations. That is the module doc's own known limit, and it is what turns the DAG the walk builds into a tree that is materialized.

Two facts fixed the shape of the fix. The exponent is in the **annotations**, so it is independent of the receiver: the same record projected off a *parameter* died identically to one projected off `ListTok()`, which rules out "reduce the projection when the value is a literal". And it is independent of `datarepr`: an eleven-field record written by hand — the generated one's shape, `R: Type` / `abs` / `inv` / two makers / four laws / `fold` / `fold_law` — aborts with no `datarepr` in the file, and drops back under the limit as soon as `fold_law` is deleted, which is the occurrence count crossing the line.

THE FIX (`crates/elab/src/gendefs.rs`): **name the projections**, the device the workaround was already using by hand. At the declaration of a dependent, unparameterized record, three Ψ definitions are registered per spine entry — `D#famⱼ` the Σ tail family, `D#projⱼ` the field, `D#tailⱼ` the pair the entry above is taken of — and each is built with *references* to the ones below it standing where the walk used to inline a node. A field's type then mentions `D#projᵢ^δ v`, one node, and the walk, the term it produces, and everything downstream of both are linear. `project_field` hands out `D#projⱼ^δ value` when there is a definition and keeps its old walk when there is not, so nothing about the fallback changed. This is `gendefs.rs`'s existing discipline verbatim (registered once at declaration under a name the surface cannot write, best-effort, gate-checked like a `fn`, δ-unfolding recovers exactly the old term) and it inherits `GenDef::TyCode`'s boundary: a **parameterized** record has no single closed lowering to name and keeps the inline walk, exponent and all. Two smaller decisions, both measured: the tail family is a **code** (`El (D#famⱼ^δ v x)`), because a code is checked rather than reduced — spelled out, each definition's statement is re-walked and every reference in it re-reduced, which cost 3.4 s against 3.0 s for the record below, and 13 s if the tails are raw `sndʲ` spines instead; and a record whose spine is not dependent is skipped entirely (`record_spine_is_dependent`), since its walk is linear already and the definitions would be all cost.

RESULTS. The issue's repro — `datarepr ListTok for List<Tok>` and `ListTok().Cons_abs(a0)(a1)(h0)` as the proof of the law it states — **checks**, in 3.2 s, where it used to abort ("memory allocation of 88 bytes failed") under a 4 GiB cap. So does the hand-written record, off a value and off a neutral receiver, at the last field as well as at the eighth. Not everything at that size fits: η at a neutral receiver (rebuilding the record from its own projections and converting it with the receiver) is now a bounded `OutOfFuel` rejection rather than an abort — a conversion cost, recorded here rather than fixed. The declaration-time cost of the definitions is real and is its own issue, [[record-projection-defs-declaration-cost]]: ~3.0 s for the eleven-field generated record, +34 s of CPU on `scripts/test --fast` (402 s → 436 s) of which ~23 s is the new regression file, i.e. ~+3% on everything that existed, concentrated in the tests that declare a generated `Repr_*`.

Tests: `crates/elab/tests/record_projection_size.rs`, 11 of them — the repro through `datarepr` and by hand, off a literal and off a neutral, at the deepest field, every one of the eleven projections identified with the clause it came from, three negative controls (a law that must not prove a neighbouring equation, a neighbouring field that must not prove this law, an unknown field), the namespace route that was the workaround still green, and the parameterized-record fallback still projecting. Suite 1579 → 1590, `scripts/bench --check` 18 comparisons within thresholds.
