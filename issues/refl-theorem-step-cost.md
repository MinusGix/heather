Datum, 2026-09-16 (release + value-metrics, `--kernel-workers 1`, tree a6ba37a), `docs/showcase/calc_parser.hthr`: the fourteen closed refl theorems — `parentheses` 1 278 ms at 1 558 queries / 8 777 steps, `executed_parentheses` 1 095 ms at 1 669 / 8 948, `nested_parentheses` 1 042 ms at 1 195 / 7 012, `wrong_close` 1 030 ms at 878 / 3 467, `missing_close` 1 014 ms at 746 / 2 791, `precedence`/`executed`/`evaluated`/`product_before_sum`/`doubled_operator`/`missing_rhs`/`missing_product_rhs` 550–600 ms each at ~700–1 400 queries and 2 500–6 000 steps — together ~12 s of the showcase's 64.5 s serial total, almost all of it kernel gate, at 0.1–0.7 ms per step and ~0.8 ms per conversion query. Elsewhere in the same run a step is ~3 µs and a query ~2 µs. The steps and queries are few; the seconds are in what each step touches: a closed run of the certified parser over five to seven tokens, through `Certified`/`rebase`/`le_trans` values that carry their proofs, on a small-step rewriter that copies branch bundles per β ([[whnf-sharing-term-growth]]'s mechanism, closed for the exponential axis, not for this constant). Unfueled costs — forcing, weakening, instantiation — are where a fuel pin cannot see. Not on the eight-worker critical path (each item is ~1 s), so second behind [[quoted-component-traversal-remainder]]. Attribute one item (`parentheses`) by profiling the kernel's time per rule, not by counters; the term-level/type-level asymmetry of [[type-level-reduction-over-computed-operands]] may be the same family.
