Designed in docs/kernel/bitvector-literal-entry-design.md §3 (K1 round 4 design pass, 2026-09-13) and deferred by its §5.2: a term the elaborator emits for a closed `[Bool; n]` literal that the evaluator reads straight into a `Val::BitRun`, with an O(1)-per-bit typing rule against the `Vec` type — about 250 lines across elab, the kernel's typing and evaluator entry, quoting, and the oracle, with the adequacy clause drafted in that doc's §6 and the tests laid out on the four axes of docs/testing-generated-terms.md (§3.5; the open-element-type item comes first, §8.6). It removes the largest floor in the *probe* table (a written-out width-64 literal costs ~3.6 ms of gate and 3.3k steps; the same value born packed is 47 µs and 0 steps, probe P5) but has **no customer**: `lib/` contains zero `[Bool; n]` literals and 281 `ofNat` sites, the reflector cannot emit one, and `function_ir_source.rs:320` requires constants to be `bitvec::ofNat(64, literal)`. Landing it as a performance change would measure flat on the certificate, which is round 2's mistake.

Triggers, either of which reopens it for implementation: a hand-written `[Bool; n]` literal on a measured path, or round 3's `BitRun`↔`Pair` oracle arm wanted reachable from the generator (§3.4 — today it is not). Check `size_of::<Term>()` before and after; box the payload rather than widen every term (§8.5).

Related: [[bitvector-literal-entry-cost]] (the round it came from), [[kernel-native-bitvector-values]] (the line), [[bitvec-ofnat-large-literals]] (closed).

## Log

- 2026-10-29: Built and landed (2026-10-29, branch claude/project-thread-qvouuv): the customer arrived, write-elf's certificates, whose every byte literal was `ofNat(8, k)` run a bit at a time. `Term::VecLit{ℓ, elem, zero, one, w, bits}` as §3 designed it: typing checks `elem`, `zero`, `one` and the range (1 ≤ w ≤ 64), nothing else; one owner of the unfolding (`term::vec_lit_unfold`) for `reduce.rs`'s `Fst`/`Snd`, the evaluator's fallback and the full chain; the evaluator reads it into one `Val::BitRun` when `elem` meets the bit criterion and `zero`/`one` are its injections, charging one fuel unit (amended from §3.3's per-bit charge: the width is capped at the word, and one unit is still below the chain's walk). Refused under `box` and layer v like the checked spellings; `RebuildChecked` writes out its chain at the quote boundary and for export. The elaborator packs a vector literal's last ≤ 64 elements when they are closed and at most two distinct terms over an element type whose whnf is a sum; write-elf emits byte literals as `from_bits(8, [...])` (immediates, header and addresses stay `ofNat`, because the lowering's own `ofNat` meets them at the spine for free, and packing them cost `e_#` about 40K steps and `header_is` about 52K on yes's certificate). Image tag 70, merkle `E_VECLIT`. DESIGN.md has the adequacy paragraph (§6, amended). Tests: 8 rows in `crates/kernel/tests/bitvec_values.rs` on the four axes (widths 1/2/63/64 both directions, swapped and constant elements, a packed tail under a pair prefix, open element type and open elements, a computed length, range/payload refusals, projections, the `box` spelling); a mutated word reading fails three of them. Not done: the generator case for the differential oracle (§3.4); the generator has no checked-spelling cases either. Measured (cloud, `scripts/bench-probes --filter cert/`, fixture regenerated): `cert/yes-pieces` 607,244 → 488,749 steps, kernel gate 852 → 557 ms in a same-box debug-binary pair (−35 %); `cert/yes-labels` flat (377K steps). Unit probes: `canonical_bytes(MovRI …)` against its bytes 9,821 → 5,168 steps. What a piece still spends is filed as [[native-encoder-ofnat-cost]]. Closing.
